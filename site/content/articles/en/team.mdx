Setting up an AI assistant is only half the work. The other half is your staff: whether they want to use it, trust its results and know what to do once admin work moves to the AI.

Without training, an AI assistant often ends up unused. Staff go back to doing everything by hand because it feels safer. Here are the steps we use when we support small businesses.

## 1. Answer the fear early

The first question in your staff's minds is usually simple: "Will I be replaced?" If it isn't answered, staff resist the change, even if they never say so.

Explain honestly what changes. The work that moves to AI is repetitive, such as copying orders or answering price questions. The work that needs people, such as selling, negotiating and looking after customers, gets more time.

<PullQuote>
  Staff who know their role is becoming more important will help the AI assistant succeed instead of holding it back.
</PullQuote>

## 2. Involve your staff in the design

The staff who do the admin work every day know the details best: which questions come up often, which mistakes happen and which exceptions need care.

Ask them to help write the list of questions and answers, the order rules and the report format. The result is more accurate, and your staff feel they own the new system.

## 3. Teach them to check the AI's work

An AI assistant can make mistakes, especially in the first weeks. Your staff need to know how to check its results and what to do when something is wrong.

- Show where the orders, invoices or summaries made by the AI are stored.
- Make a short checklist, for example: quantities, delivery address and total price.
- Agree on how to report problems: if the AI answers wrongly, who is told and how the data is fixed.

<Callout tone="safe" title="Mistakes are material for improvement">
  A wrong answer usually means some data is missing. Note the mistake, complete the data, and the assistant answers
  better next time.
</Callout>

## 4. Give them a clear new role

The time that used to go into admin work needs to be filled with clear work. Otherwise staff get confused and fall back into old habits.

New roles we often see working:

- contacting regular customers who haven't ordered in a while;
- following up with people who asked but didn't order;
- preparing weekly promotion ideas and content;
- learning new products so they can offer more complete packages.

These roles need new skills too, such as sales techniques, negotiation and reading simple sales data. Set aside time to train them.

<Band title="Short practice, but regular">
  A short weekly session works better than one long training. Discuss one real case from that week, such as a chat that
  was handled well or an order that almost went wrong.
</Band>

## 5. Measure and celebrate progress

Set a few simple measures from the start and review them with your staff every month:

1. how many hours a week no longer go into admin work;
2. how many regular customers were contacted again;
3. how many sales came from staff follow-ups;
4. which mistakes still happen often and how to fix them.

When there is progress, tell your staff. Small recognition shows them that the change benefits them too.

## In short

Training your staff to work with AI means answering their fear, involving them early, teaching them to check the results, giving them new roles and measuring progress together. This way the AI assistant doesn't replace your staff. It helps them move up.
