Customer chats are the front door of your sales. Quick, clear replies turn questions into orders, while slow replies send customers to another shop. That is why many business owners are looking at AI assistants to answer chats.

An AI assistant can help a lot. But not every business needs one right now. This article helps you judge your own situation.

## Signs you need an AI assistant

- **Chats pile up at busy times.** Staff reply one by one, and customers who write later wait a long time.
- **The questions are similar.** Most chats ask about prices, stock, schedules or how to order.
- **Chats arrive outside working hours.** Evening questions are answered the next morning, when the customer has already bought elsewhere.
- **Staff have no time for anything else.** Replying to chats takes the whole day, so preparing orders or promotions gets delayed.

If two or more of these sound familiar, your staff will most likely feel the benefit of an AI assistant quickly.

## When it is better to wait

- **You still get few chats.** If your staff can still answer every chat quickly, the money is better spent on bringing in customers.
- **Every chat is different and needs negotiation.** For example custom projects with prices worked out each time. Here an AI assistant helps most with recording, not answering.
- **Your business data isn't organised yet.** An AI assistant answers from your business data. If prices and rules aren't written down, organise them first.

## What a good AI assistant looks like

A good AI assistant does more than answer fast. Make sure of three things:

1. **It answers from your business data.** Prices, stock and schedules come from a list you manage yourself, so the answers are always correct.
2. **It knows when to stop.** Questions it has no answer for, complaints and special requests go to your staff together with the conversation.
3. **It speaks in your business's voice.** Greetings, sentence length and wording match the way your staff usually reply.

<PullQuote>A good AI assistant knows when to hand the conversation to a person.</PullQuote>

## What to prepare

- A list of products or services with prices.
- Key rules: opening hours, delivery fees, payment methods, return or cancellation policy.
- The questions that come in most often and the answers your staff usually give.
- Who receives conversations that are passed on, and at what times.

## Measuring the results

After the assistant has run for a few weeks, look at these numbers:

- how long customers wait on average for the first reply;
- what share of conversations is passed to staff;
- how many chats end in an order;
- what work your staff now have time for.

<Band title="Test with real chats first">
  Before the assistant answers every customer, test it with sample chats from your own business. You will see which
  questions are already answered well and which need more data.
</Band>

If the numbers improve and your staff have time for other work, your assistant is doing its job. If not, the problem is usually incomplete data, not the assistant.
