Many business owners know that posting regularly on social media helps sales. The problem is that ideas often run out by the second week. Content gets posted half-heartedly, or not at all.

AI can help with the most tiring part: gathering material, working out ideas and drafting captions. The final decision stays with you or your staff. Here is a weekly routine you can use.

## The material is already in your business

The best content ideas rarely come from trends far from your business. They are usually already in your daily conversations:

- **Customer questions.** Questions that come in often can become Q&A posts.
- **Reviews and testimonials.** Stories from happy customers are the most trusted proof.
- **Products that sell well or just arrived.** Sales data shows which products deserve attention.
- **Important dates.** Payday, school holidays, public holidays and seasons change what customers need.

An AI assistant can gather this material every week from chat records, reviews and sales summaries, then put it into one list.

## The weekly routine

1. **Monday: gather material.** The AI summarises the previous week's questions, reviews and best sellers.
2. **Tuesday: work out ideas.** From that summary, the AI suggests content ideas for one week, each with a goal, such as introducing a new product or answering a common doubt.
3. **Wednesday: choose and adjust.** You or your staff pick the best ideas and adjust the wording to your business's style.
4. **Thursday: prepare photos and design.** Use real photos of your products and premises. AI can help tidy up photos or lay out a carousel.
5. **Friday: schedule.** All content is scheduled for the following week.

<PullQuote>AI prepares the material and drafts. Your business's voice still comes from the people inside it.</PullQuote>

## Keeping your own voice

Content written by AI without direction tends to sound the same as everyone else's. To keep your content sounding like your business:

- write down example greetings and the tone your business usually uses;
- list the words and terms your customers often use;
- decide what must never be written, such as promises you can't keep;
- ask the staff member closest to your customers to read drafts before posting.

<Callout tone="safe" title="Always check the facts">
  Prices, stock, promotion dates and product claims must be checked by a person. AI can write convincing sentences even
  when the data is wrong.
</Callout>

## Example plan for one week

For a cake shop, the routine above could produce a plan like this:

- **Monday:** a photo of last week's most-ordered cake, with a short story about its ingredients.
- **Wednesday:** an answer to the most common question, for example how many days a cake keeps.
- **Friday:** a testimonial from a customer who ordered for a family event.
- **Sunday:** an announcement of a special menu for the next weekend.

Four posts a week are enough to keep your business visible, as long as they are useful to customers.

<Band title="Start small">
  If regular posting feels heavy, start with two posts a week. Add more once the routine runs smoothly and you know
  which content your customers like best.
</Band>

## Seeing the results

After a month, look at which posts were saved, shared or prompted customers to ask by chat the most. Use those posts as examples for the following weeks. That way the AI also learns which ideas suit your customers.
