Some work comes back every single day. Customers ask the same price questions, orders from chats are copied into a book, invoices are made one by one, and staff still have to remind customers who haven't paid. All of this work matters. The problem is that it eats the hours your staff could spend serving customers and finding new sales.

An AI assistant can take over most of this repetitive work. Here are the five admin tasks we see most often in small businesses, and how an AI assistant handles each of them.

## 1. Answering the same questions again and again

Prices, opening hours, address, delivery fees and stock are asked about every day. An AI assistant reads your product list and business rules, then answers customers at any time, including outside working hours.

A good assistant doesn't make answers up. If a question isn't covered by your business data, the assistant passes the conversation to a staff member, together with the chat so far. Your staff don't have to ask the customer again.

<Callout tone="tip" title="Start with a short list">
  Write down the 20 questions that come in most often over one week. That list usually covers most of the daily chats.
</Callout>

## 2. Recording orders from chats

In many businesses, orders that arrive by chat are written again into a book or a spreadsheet. This is slow and error-prone: quantities get mixed up or delivery addresses are incomplete.

An AI assistant takes the item names, quantities, delivery address and customer notes from the conversation and writes them into the order table. If something is missing, the assistant asks the customer before the order is recorded. Your staff only check the table and process the order.

## 3. Creating and sending invoices

Invoices made by hand are often sent late because staff are busy serving customers. With automation, invoices are created from the order data already recorded: customer name, items, total and the account to pay into. The invoice is then sent to the customer in a tidy, consistent format.

Because the data comes from the same table, the numbers on the invoice always match the order. Nobody has to add them up again.

## 4. Reminding customers about unpaid bills

Reminding customers to pay often feels awkward, so it gets put off. An AI assistant sends polite reminders on a schedule, for example one day before the due date, on the due date and a few days after.

Payments that have arrived are marked in the table, so customers who have paid aren't reminded. If a bill is still unpaid after several reminders, the case goes to the owner or the staff member in charge.

## 5. Summarising daily sales

Many owners only see their sales figures at the end of the month, when the records have piled up. A sales summary automation sums up every evening: the day's total, the best-selling products and the payments still outstanding. The summary is sent to the owner in one short message.

With daily numbers, owners spot problems early, such as a product that is selling less or stock that is about to run out.

<PullQuote>
  Admin work doesn't disappear. It moves to the AI assistant, and your staff move to work that brings in revenue.
</PullQuote>

## What stays with your staff

An AI assistant suits work with clear rules that repeats. Some work is still better handled by people:

- special prices, discounts and negotiations with large customers;
- customer complaints that need empathy and a solution;
- the final check before goods are shipped;
- promotion ideas and relationships with regular customers.

This is where your staff are most valuable. The time they used to spend copying data can go into selling, negotiating and looking after customers.

<Band title="Measure the time first">
  For one week, ask your staff to note how many minutes each of the tasks above takes. These numbers help you choose
  which task is most worth automating first.
</Band>

## How to start

1. Note the repetitive tasks for one week, including who does them and how long they take.
2. Choose one task that comes up most often and has the clearest rules.
3. Prepare the data, such as your price list, invoice format and customer list.
4. Test the assistant with real conversations and orders before using it fully.
5. Once it runs well, move on to the next task.

Starting with one task makes it easier for your staff to adjust, and you see the results before adding more automation.
