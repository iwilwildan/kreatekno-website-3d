Many small businesses are busy with customers, yet the owner is still puzzled at the end of the month: where did the money go? The cause is often the same. Sales are recorded in several places, daily costs aren't recorded, and everything is only counted once the records have piled up.

Recording sales every day isn't hard in itself. What is hard is doing it consistently while the business is busy. This is where automation helps.

## Why daily records matter

Monthly numbers show what has already happened. Daily numbers show what is happening now. With daily records, you can:

- see the busiest days and hours, then plan staff schedules;
- notice products that sell less before their stock piles up;
- find cash differences sooner, while the day is still easy to remember;
- read profit and loss every week, not only every month.

## What to record

For a small business, a daily record needs only four things:

1. **Sales:** date, product, quantity and price.
2. **Payment method:** cash, transfer or other methods, so cash in hand and the bank account can be matched.
3. **Daily costs:** raw materials, delivery fees and other small expenses.
4. **Special notes:** discounts, returns or orders not yet paid.

<Callout tone="tip" title="Keep business and personal money apart">
  Automation helps most when the business account is separate from your personal account. Without that, the profit and
  loss figures will always be mixed up.
</Callout>

## How automatic recording works

Automation doesn't change the way you sell. It takes data from the places where transactions already happen:

1. **Orders from chats** are recorded in the sales table by the AI assistant when the customer confirms the order.
2. **Cashier transactions** are copied from the cashier app into the same table every day.
3. **Daily costs** are sent by staff as a short message, for example "bought 2 sacks of flour, 340 thousand", and the AI turns it into a row in the cost table.
4. **An evening summary** goes to the owner: total sales, total costs and orders still unpaid.

<PullQuote>
  Tidy records don't wait for the end of the month. They build themselves from every day's transactions.
</PullQuote>

## Reading a simple profit and loss

At the end of each week, the summary can include a simple calculation:

- **sales** for the week;
- minus the **cost of the goods** sold;
- minus **operating costs**, such as wages, rent, electricity and delivery;
- the result is the week's **estimated profit**.

This isn't an official financial statement, but it answers the important questions: did the business make a profit this week, and which products did the profit come from?

<Band title="Let the numbers ask questions">
  If sales go up but estimated profit goes down, look for the cause in your costs. If a product sells well but its
  margin is thin, its price may need a review.
</Band>

## Common mistakes

- **Only recording sales.** Without costs, sales look good even when the profit is small.
- **Recording in many places.** A notebook, notes on a phone and chat messages that are never combined.
- **Waiting for free time to record.** Free time rarely comes in a busy business.

## What stays your decision

Automation helps you record and summarise. The decisions stay with you: raising prices, cutting costs or stocking more of the most profitable products. For tax reports and official financial statements, keep working with an accountant or tax consultant.
